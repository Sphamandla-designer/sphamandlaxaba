Design Process

Four stages, in order — from how the work happens today to something developers can build.

01 · Map

Understand the current state

Workflow analysis, existing process documentation and current- and future-state journeys — plus a stakeholder map showing where trust and handoffs actually break. All of it before the first screen.

02 · Decide

Agree it before designing it

Assumptions become decisions logged against a named business owner, worked through in stakeholder sessions rather than settled on my judgment alone. That register is what the design is built on.

03 · Design

Principles before screens

Design principles first, so the hard calls have something to answer to. Then information architecture, flows, and the states most work skips — empty, offline, low-confidence, error.

04 · Prove

Validate, then hand over

Working prototypes put in front of the people who have to use and fund the work, then component specs, tokens and a handoff session — so what ships is what was designed.

About

I design the parts of a product where the workflow is the hard part.

I'm Sphamandla Xaba, a Product Designer working across UX, UI and AI UX from Johannesburg. Over three years I've designed websites, mobile products and SaaS platforms — taking work from discovery and research through to developer handoff.

Most of what I do sits behind the interface: onboarding that has to earn trust, operational dashboards people live in all day, AI-assisted review where someone still has to be accountable for the result. The screens are the easy part.

My job is to make complexity legible — not to pretend it isn't there.
