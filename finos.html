FINOS · AI FINANCIAL OPERATING SYSTEM

Designing an AI financial operating system for better decisions — not just better dashboards.

FINOS is a concept financial operating system exploring how AI can help people understand their financial position, model possible futures, evaluate trade-offs and take action — while keeping humans in control of consequential decisions.

03 — THE PROBLEM

Financial decisions are rarely about one number.

A person's financial reality is distributed across accounts, transactions, investments, bills, goals, documents and future commitments.

When making a consequential decision, the user has to mentally assemble all of this context before they can even begin asking: What should I do?

THE PROBLEM HAS THREE LAYERS

01 — Fragmented context

Financial information often exists as separate pieces. The user becomes the integration layer — holding seven sources in their head long enough to form one picture.

INSIGHT

The system needs to construct the financial context before asking the user to make a decision.

02 — Complex trade-offs

A consequential financial decision isn't represented by a single metric. It involves liquidity, cash flow, debt, risk, goals, time horizon and opportunity cost at once — and changing one assumption can change the entire recommendation.

INSIGHT

Instead of presenting one answer, allow users to model and compare possible futures.

03 — AI creates a new trust problem

AI can produce a recommendation in seconds. But when the consequence is significant, speed is not the scarce resource — grounds for acting are.

INSIGHT

Explainability needs to be part of the decision itself — not an optional AI feature.

04 — THE DESIGN CHALLENGE

The challenge wasn't simply building an AI financial assistant.

It was deciding how much control AI should have.

THE AUTONOMY SPECTRUM

LOW AUTONOMY

Manual work

The user gathers, calculates and decides. Full agency, no leverage.

ASSISTED

AI prepares and recommends

Useful, but the reasoning stays opaque and the record stays thin.

  2. AI Analyses
  3. AI Recommends
  4. HUMAN Evaluates
  5. HUMAN Challenges
  6. HUMAN Approves
  7. SYSTEM Acts
  8. SYSTEM Records

HIGH AUTONOMY

AI acts automatically

Fast and unaccountable. The consequence arrives before the reasoning does.

FINOS sits deliberately between manual financial management and fully autonomous financial AI — the AI carries the analysis, the human carries the consequence.

THE HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP MODEL

AI CAPABILITY

  1. Understand Assemble the position across accounts, commitments and portfolio.
  2. Analyse Model the scenarios and the trade-offs between them.
  3. Recommend State a position with reasoning, evidence, risks and confidence.
HUMAN CONTROL

HUMAN AUTHORITY & SYSTEM RECORD

  1. ReviewInspect the reasoning before the outcome. HUMAN
  2. ChallengeChange an assumption and force a re-run. HUMAN
  3. ApproveAuthorise explicitly, attributed to a named role. HUMAN
  4. ActionExecute only what was approved, nothing adjacent. SYSTEM
  5. AuditRecord what AI proposed and what the human decided. SYSTEM

The line is not a handoff between two products — it is the point in the architecture where capability stops and authority begins.

How might we help people make consequential financial decisions with AI — without requiring them to blindly trust it?

This question became the north star for FINOS — influencing its decision architecture, AI interactions, approval model and auditability.

AI should increase decision capability — not remove decision agency.

05 — RESEARCH

Before designing the system, I needed to understand the decision.

Rather than starting with a dashboard or a set of AI features, I framed the research around how people understand financial situations, evaluate consequential decisions and decide whether an AI recommendation deserves their trust.

FINOS used exploratory, self-initiated research to identify recurring decision-making needs and inform the product direction.

This research was directional rather than statistically representative. The goal was to generate and prioritise design hypotheses, not to establish population-level conclusions.

THEME 04

Automation needs proportional control.

Not every financial action carries the same consequence. The appropriate level of human involvement should depend on what the system is about to do.

LOW CONSEQUENCE

AI can automate

Categorising a transaction. Reversible, low value, high volume.

MEDIUM CONSEQUENCE

AI prepares
Human reviews

Reclassifying a record or flagging a duplicate. Correctable, but visible.

HIGH CONSEQUENCE

AI prepares
Human approves
System executes

Moving money or recording a decision. Hard to unwind, so never automatic.

INSIGHT

The level of human control should increase with the consequence of the action.

CONFIDENCE MODEL

HIGH CONFIDENCE

Every supporting factor holds and nothing counted against the recommendation.

MODERATE CONFIDENCE

FINOS still recommends, but at least one risk is carried — and it is named.

NOT ENOUGH INFORMATION

FINOS declines to assess, states what is missing and what would improve confidence.

Confidence is not the same as trust. It is a signal that helps users understand how strongly FINOS can support its recommendation — which is why it always appears next to the reasoning, never on its own.

Decision clarity

WHAT HAPPENED

When the recommended option was the most prominent thing on screen, it could be read as the answer rather than as advice — the point where the user stops evaluating and starts complying.

EVIDENCE

Prototype evaluation · exploratory

WHY IT MATTERS

On a consequential financial decision, a recommendation mistaken for a verdict transfers authorship of the decision to the system. Nothing in the interface should let that happen quietly.

DESIGN RESPONSE

The recommendation now names itself as a recommendation, carries its confidence in the same block, and the approval step requires the user to acknowledge that FINOS recommended rather than authorised the decision.
